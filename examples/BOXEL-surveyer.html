Mass-Code Geometry

nested cubes inside a 1280 ly procedural sector
Mass code describes the ZAMS mass category of the primary star. It is useful for targeting stellar populations, but it is not a promise about the present-day total mass or contents of every individual system.
Coordinates define a complete nested boxel hierarchy. A procedural 1280 ly sector contains 1 H cube, 8 G cubes, 64 F, 512 E, 4,096 D, 32,768 C, 262,144 B and 2,097,152 A cubes. Every XYZ point falls in exactly one cube at each H→A scale. Each parent contains eight children because each axis is halved.

How to Read a Name

procedural naming grammar
Pyrie Thae XO-Z d13-12

Sector: Pyrie Thae
boxel letters: XO-Z
mass code: d
high boxel index N1: 13
system sequence N2: 12

When N1 is zero it is omitted, so a system can look like Traileia AA-A h0.

Survey Discipline

what the tab can and cannot know
Spansh backfill is reference data, not exploration credit. Known systems imported from Spansh are blue in the cube/sequence grid until your journal records a visit. Visiting one promotes it to the normal explored color while retaining both provenance flags.
The final sequence number is the important denominator for a full survey. Public databases are incomplete by construction because undiscovered systems have never been submitted. BOXEL therefore learns from your journal, the in-game Galaxy Map and your explicit unavailable/range confirmations.
Boxel-wide correlations such as helium-rich gas giants are shown only from your measured sample. The tab reports sample count, mean, spread and range rather than assuming every boxel has a special “trait.”
* Unfootfalled means Elite reported WasFootfalled:false when the body was scanned. BOXEL treats that as an opportunity indicator, not proof that you personally achieved first footfall.

Spansh backfill is always manual. Searches use the procedural system-name prefix plus the physical XYZ cube whenever BOXEL can identify the exact boxel; cross-mass searches fall back to a sector-name prefix until a matching system identifies the exact boxel. Each click remains chunk-limited with mass-code-specific pacing/cooldowns.
Full backfill is deliberately manual because a long journal archive can contain a very large number of records. Startup only replays the current journal.
